Operations

Route planning starts with people, not just maps

A practical framework for matching routes, shifts and employee needs.

Buus Editorial · 3 min read

Understand the journey first

A useful route plan starts with where people live, when their shifts begin and how they reach the pickup point. Review these inputs together before drawing a route. A convenient stop on a map may still be difficult to reach on foot.

Balance the operation

Compare travel time, vehicle capacity and arrival windows. Look at individual routes as well as the whole network: improving one route can create a longer connection or a tighter arrival window elsewhere.

Keep improving

Review actual trip times, occupancy and passenger feedback on a regular schedule. Use that information to identify a specific adjustment, test it and compare the result with the original plan.
